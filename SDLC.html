Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)

The Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is a structured process used for developing software applications. Explain the stages of the SDLC below, in detail:

Planning

In the planning phase, the goals, objectives and requirements of the project are decided and documented. This will also take into account the business case for the project and how to meet this. When all of these are documented, an initial plan of the project will be produced which can then be followed until completion. This stage may involve business analysts, project managers, team leaders, stakeholders or senior management.

Requirements

Once the plan is finalised and approved, the requirements for the product can then be explored further. This will involve gathering user requirements, testing the feasibility of the work and possibly conducting market research around the project. This may result in the creation of a requirements specification documents such as an SRS. Those most involved in this phase may include business analysts, stakeholders, product owners and possibly end users.

Design

The design phase involves defining the project's architecture. Parts of the system that may be outlined at this time may include navigation, user interfaces or database architecture among other areas. This may culminate in a prototype being developed to get feedback on the initial design and will involve software developers and technical leads.

Development

In this phase, the team will start to code and build the project based on the previously stated guidelines. The team involved at this point will mostly consist of software developers, any technical leads to oversee the project and other DevOps personnel. This may involve building additional systems that are necessary for the software to function correctly.

Testing

Once the initial development is complete, the software can then be tested. The testing will usually be conducted by QA testers, who can then give feedback on the project. Testing will allow the team to discover any bugs, errors and vulnerabilites within the software and can give the opportunity for the developers to resolve ny problems before the project is live.

Deployment

Once the software has been tested it can then be deployed to the production environment for the end user to access it. The software may be deployed in phases to allow for users to give feedback before a full version is released to the public. This stage may involve systems administrators, software developers and possibly release managers.

Maintenance

Once deployed and live, the software then needs to be maintained in order to keep it competitive. This may involve new developments in the software, bug fixes, security patches or simply optimisations. This will be overseen by developers and IT support staff.
